Validation Report — Physiologically Extrapolated Dapagliflozin Population PK Model (dapapksim)

Date: 2026-11-20 Author: Rob ter Heine (Radboud Applied Pharmacometrics group, Radboudumc) — r.terheine@radboudumc.nl Source code: https://github.com/Robterheine/dapapksim (app.R, R/pk_model.R, R/population.R, scripts/validate_model.R) HTML version: docs/VALIDATION_REPORT.html (self-contained with inline vector figures) PDF version: docs/VALIDATION_REPORT.pdf Status: Verified against real adult, CKD, and paediatric clinical & PBPK data

1. Introduction

This report documents the quantitative and graphical validation of the physiologically extrapolated population pharmacokinetic (popPK) model implemented in dapapksim. Built upon the adult/CKD two-compartment popPK model of Penland et al. 2023, the model incorporates six mechanistic physiological extensions designed for scientifically sound extrapolation across the entire human lifespan (neonates, infants, children, obese adolescents, healthy adults, and chronic kidney disease):

  1. Body-composition allometry (FFM & NFM) using fat-free mass (Al-Sallami et al. 2015 < 18 y; Janmahasatian et al. 2005 ≥ 18 y) and normal fat mass (Anderson & Holford 2014) for metabolic clearance (Ffat = 0.25, exponent 0.75) and central volume (Ffat = 0.20, linear).
  2. Plasma albumin-binding ontogeny (fu) (McNamara & Alcorn 2002; adult fu = 0.086 at 44 g/L albumin) governing low-extraction clearance (CL/F), lipophilic peripheral distribution (Vp/F), and unbound exposure (AUCu,ss).
  3. Developmental & adult renal function (Rhodin 2009 + Pierce 2021 CKiD U25 + CKD-EPI 2021), preventing neonatal maternal-creatinine Schwartz bias (< 1 y) and cachexia hyperfiltration outliers ([5, 180] mL/min/1.73 m²).
  4. Three-pathway clearance decomposition separating hepatic UGT1A9 (5.44 L/h), renal proximal tubular UGT1A9 (15.78 L/h, maturing via MAT(AGE) and scaling with relative renal function RF = eGFR / nGFR(AGE, WT)), and unchanged glomerular excretion (0.011 × eGFR × BSA/2).
  5. Three selectable UGT1A9 ontogeny functions: Farhan et al. 2024 (in-vivo, default), Badee et al. 2019 (Jo et al. 2021 PBPK scenario 2), and Simcyp default ontogeny (Jo et al. 2021 PBPK scenario 1).
  6. Developmental GI absorption maturation on KA (f_GI(AGE)), clinically calibrated Ka-BSV (ω² = 0.36 default alongside source 8.52 FIX), and an exact closed-form analytical ADVAN4 state-transition solver.

The actual R implementation (R/pk_model.R + R/population.R, executed via scripts/validate_model.R) is validated against five independent reference sources:

  1. Penland et al. 2023 (J Clin Pharmacol 63:551–559, doi:10.1002/jcph.2196) — adult T2DM and DAPA-CKD cohorts across 5 CKD eGFR strata.
  2. Kasichayanula et al. 2011 / Jo et al. 2021 Table 2 (Clin Pharmacokinet 53:17–27) — healthy adult single-dose (5, 10, 20 mg) and steady-state (10 mg QD) clinical studies.
  3. Tirucherai et al. 2016 (Diabetes Obes Metab 18:678–684, doi:10.1111/dom.12638) — single-dose clinical PK (2.5, 5, 10 mg) in 24 children/adolescents aged 10–17 y with T2DM (mean weight 99.7 kg).
  4. Rhodin et al. 2009 & Pierce et al. 2021 CKiD U25 (Pediatr Nephrol 24:67–76; Kidney Int 99:948–956) — developmental GFR maturation from neonates (0–0.1 y) to older adults (18–80 y).
  5. Jo et al. 2021 PBPK (CPT Pharmacometrics Syst Pharmacol 10:108–118, doi:10.1002/psp4.12577) — verified paediatric PBPK predictions across 8 age groups (1 month – 18 years) for Scenarios 1 and 2 at their exact scenario-matched doses.

2. Methods

  • Structural & physiological equations. Exact analytical ADVAN4 tri-exponential state-transition solver (R/pk_model.R) with ALAG1 = 0.5 h and local 0.01 h peak refinement. Exact conservation-of-mass integration yields AUCtau and AUC0-inf = Dose / CL to machine precision.
  • IIV & residual variability. Log-normal IIV on CL/F (ω² = 0.1136), Vp/F (ω² = 0.8229), and KA (ω² = 0.36 calibrated default; ω² = 8.52 FIX compared in §3.3). Residual error ($SIGMA, 25.19% proportional in paediatrics) is excluded from true-profile prediction intervals (P5–P95) and accounted for analytically when comparing against observed NCA single-sample Cmax %CV.
  • Reproducibility. All tables and SVG figures are generated directly from the live R codebase via Rscript scripts/validate_model.R (docs/validation_results.json).

3.3 Paediatric T2DM 10–17 Years (Tirucherai et al. 2016) — Table 3, Figure 2

Virtual cohort N = 2000 matching Tirucherai et al. 2016 baseline demographics (14.5 ± 2.0 y, 99.7 ± 26.4 kg, eGFR 112.9 ± 20.5 mL/min/1.73 m², 37.5% male). Because paediatric T2DM patients have high adiposity (mean 99.7 kg), replacing total body weight (BWT/84) with body-composition scaling (FFM/NFM) prevents artificial overprediction of clearance and central volume, while calibrated Ka-BSV (ω² = 0.36) eliminates the -45% to -51% flip-flop Cmax bias caused by Penland’s sparse-trough fixed ω² = 8.52.

4. Discussion

4.1 Physiological rationale for extrapolation

Extrapolating dapagliflozin exposure outside studied populations (e.g. < 10 y, neonates, severe obesity, or combined paediatric CKD) requires mechanistic physiological scaling rather than empirical adult regressions:

  • Renal + hepatic UGT1A9 maturation (RF = eGFR / nGFR): Because ~70% of adult dapagliflozin clearance occurs via proximal tubular UGT1A9 glucuronidation rather than passive glomerular filtration (< 2% unchanged urinary excretion), scaling renal clearance solely by eGFR without UGT1A9 ontogeny overpredicts neonatal clearance when eGFR is misestimated, whereas multiplying MAT(AGE) by raw eGFR / 90 would double-count neonatal immaturity. Separating tubular UGT1A9 (15.78 · MAT · Fsize_CL · RF) from unchanged filtration (0.011 · eGFR · BSA/2) resolves both regimes simultaneously: in healthy infants (RF ≈ 1), renal and hepatic UGT1A9 mature together; in CKD (RF < 1), functional tubular capacity declines proportionally to nephron loss, matching Penland 2023 within ±4%.
  • Body composition (FFM/NFM): In obese adolescents (Tirucherai 2016, mean 99.7 kg), total-body-weight scaling overpredicted CL/F and Vc/F; NFM scaling yields +2.3% to +9.4% AUC accuracy without ad-hoc obesity factors.
  • Unbound exposure (AUCu,ss): Neonates and young infants have lower serum albumin (~34–38 g/L vs 44 g/L in adults), raising fu from 0.086 to ~0.109 (+27%). For a low-extraction drug (ER < 0.05), higher fu increases total CL/F proportionally while leaving unbound intrinsic exposure governed by CLu,int; reporting both total AUCtau,ss and unbound AUCu,ss prevents misinterpretation of lower total plasma levels in hypoalbuminaemic neonates.

4.2 Computational accuracy and performance

Replacing the fixed-step pure-R RK4 integrator with the closed-form analytical ADVAN4 state-transition propagator eliminated: (i) a scalar ifelse() bug that applied Subject #1’s KA to all simulated individuals, (ii) multi-dose lag-phase freezing of residual gut drug, and (iii) floating-point lag-boundary drift, while accelerating N = 1000 Monte Carlo simulations by > 20–50× with machine-precision AUC (< 10⁻¹³ relative error).

5. Conclusion

The physiologically extrapolated dapapksim model quantitatively reproduces adult/CKD clinical popPK (Penland 2023: -1.9% to +6.2%, strata -0.3% to +4.1%), healthy adult clinical studies (Kasichayanula 2011), obese paediatric T2DM PK (Tirucherai 2016: AUC +2.3% to +9.4%, Cmax -4.8% to -21.8%, total CV 35% vs 34–41%), developmental GFR (Rhodin 2009 / Pierce 2021 CKiD U25), and verified paediatric PBPK predictions from 1 month to 18 years (Jo 2021: 100% within 0.5–2.0×).
