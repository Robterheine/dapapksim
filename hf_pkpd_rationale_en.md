### Methodological rationale for PK, PD, and therapeutic window assumptions

In the absence of published paediatric phase-III dose–response trials of dapagliflozin in heart failure, this module combines **adult clinical exposure anchors** with **mechanistic developmental physiology** in accordance with the **ICH E11A** paediatric extrapolation framework. The table below specifies the clinical-pharmacological rationale, empirical reference sources, and remaining uncertainty in young children for each model assumption.

---

#### 1. Overview of the 9 model assumptions, evidence base, and uncertainties

| No. | Domain | Model assumption | Clinical-pharmacological rationale | Primary references | Uncertainty & clinical implication in young children |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Exposure metric (`AUCu,24h,ss`)** | Target thresholds are defined on **unbound** steady-state 24-hour exposure (`AUCu,24h,ss`), rather than total plasma concentration. | Dapagliflozin is `91.4%` bound to serum albumin (`fu_adult = 0.086` at `44 g/L`) and acts as a low-extraction drug (`ER < 0.05`). Total clearance \(CL/F\) scales proportionally with \(f_u\), whereas intrinsic unbound clearance \(CL_u/F\) governs pharmacologically active exposure. Hypoalbuminaemia (neonatal ontogeny, venous congestion, cardiac cachexia, or Fontan PLE) lowers total plasma concentrations without lowering \(C_u(t)\). | Kasichayanula 2014; McNamara & Alcorn 2002; FDA NDA 202293 | **Low uncertainty (high confidence).** Prevents a systematic `15–40%` upward dosing error in hypoalbuminaemia or when interpreting total-concentration TDM. |
| **2** | **Lower target bound (`20 ng·h/mL`)** | Lower bound for effective exposure and conservative initiation target set at `AUCu,24h,ss = 20 ng·h/mL`. | Matches median unbound exposure at **adult 5 mg once daily** (`19.2 ng·h/mL` at `eGFR 90`) and approaches the `5th percentile` of adults on `10 mg` with normal renal function (`23.7 ng·h/mL`; `275 × 0.086`). Already yields `~79%` mean 24-hour luminal SGLT2 occupancy and `~80%` of maximal TGF response. | Penland 2023 (Table S4); Kroonen 2020; Parkinson 2018 | **Moderate.** Because `5 mg` was not prospectively tested against `10 mg` in DAPA-HF, `20 ng·h/mL` serves as a rational *starting anchor* prior to titration toward the `10 mg` maintenance midpoint. |
| **3** | **Target midpoint (`38–48`) & upper bound (`75 ng·h/mL`)** | Maintenance target aligned with adult `10 mg` heart failure exposure (`target midpoint 42 ng·h/mL`, window upper bound `75 ng·h/mL`). | In DAPA-HF and DELIVER, `10 mg QD` consistently reduced cardiovascular death and HF hospitalisation. Median `AUCu,24h,ss` is `38.5 ng·h/mL` at `eGFR 90` (`447 × 0.086`) and `48.2 ng·h/mL` for the typical DAPA-HF profile (`66 y`, `eGFR 66`; `560 × 0.086`). The `P95` in mild-to-moderate CKD (`eGFR 60–89`) lies right at `74.5 ng·h/mL` (`866 × 0.086`). | McMurray 2019 (DAPA-HF); Penland 2023 (Table S4) | **High for adult PK anchor; moderate for disease extrapolation.** Assumes comparable exposure–response across paediatric HF aetiologies (DCM, congenital heart disease, Fontan). |
| **4** | **Safety ceiling (`90 ng·h/mL`)** | Upper exposure cap for dose selection keeping population `P95` below `90 ng·h/mL`. | Matches the **median unbound exposure (`87.7 ng·h/mL`)** in severe chronic kidney disease (`eGFR 15–29 mL/min/1.73m²`) on `10 mg QD` (`1020 × 0.086`). Up to this exposure, `10 mg` proved clinically safe in DAPA-CKD and DAPA-HF without dose reduction; above it, long-term safety data are limited. | Penland 2023 (Table S4); Heerspink 2020 (DAPA-CKD) | **Pragmatic PK ceiling.** Note: metabolic and haemodynamic adverse events in infants (hypoglycaemia, euglycaemic ketosis, prerenal AKI) are PD-mediated and may occur *within* the therapeutic window. |
| **5** | **Three-pathway clearance decomposition** | Decomposition into hepatic UGT1A9 (`5.44 L/h`), renal proximal tubular UGT1A9 (`15.78 L/h`, scaled by both `MAT` and `RF = eGFR/nGFR`), and passive filtration (`0.99 L/h`). | Less than `2%` is excreted unchanged in urine; the steep `eGFR` dependence arises from renal proximal tubular glucuronidation to inactive D3OG. Scaling by age-normalised relative renal function `RF` prevents double-counting low neonatal GFR in healthy infants (`RF ≈ 1`) while preserving clearance decline in CKD or cardiorenal hypoperfusion (`RF < 1`). | Kasichayanula 2014; Penland 2023; Jo 2021 | **High confidence `> 1 y`; moderate uncertainty `< 6 mo`.** Synchronous developmental maturation of renal tubular and hepatic UGT1A9 is mechanistically plausible but has limited direct clinical verification in neonates. |
| **6** | **UGT1A9 ontogeny (`Farhan` vs. `Simcyp s1`)** | Three literature functions span fast *in-vivo* maturation (`TM50 = 0.09 y`) to conservative slow PBPK ontogeny (`TM50 = 1.16 y`). | Clinical *in-vivo* propofol data (`Farhan 2024`) indicate rapid postnatal maturation, whereas *in-vitro* PBPK (`Simcyp s1`, Jo 2021) is substantially slower. Evaluating all three simultaneously on the same virtual cohort quantifies structural ontogeny uncertainty. | Farhan 2024; Badee 2019; Jo 2021 | **Dominant source of PK uncertainty below 6 months (`3–4×` fold difference).** Supports cautious initiation at the `Simcyp s1` starting dose in young infants. |
| **7** | **Allometry (`FFM`/`NFM`) & oedema separation** | Metabolic clearance scales allometrically (`exponent 0.75`) with *normal fat mass* computed from **euvolaemic dry weight**; extracellular oedema expands `V2` only. | Young children have higher liver and kidney mass per kg body weight (`~0.18–0.20 mg/kg` needed to match adult `10 mg ≈ 0.12 mg/kg` exposure). Extracellular fluid overload does not add functional UGT1A9 enzyme mass. | Al-Sallami 2015; Anderson & Holford 2008; Tirucherai 2016 | **High confidence.** Validated against adolescent PK data (`10–17 y`, Tirucherai 2016) and paediatric PBPK simulations (`1 mo – 18 y`, Jo 2021). |
| **8** | **Heart failure haemodynamics & enteral bioavailability (`F1`)** | Reduction factors for cardiorenal perfusion (`rf_factor`), hepatic venous congestion (`cl_hep_factor`), and relative bioavailability (`f1_rel`). | Adult HFrEF patients exhibit `~20%` higher systemic exposure than T2DM cohorts; moderate-to-severe hepatic impairment increases dapagliflozin `AUC` by `+40–67%`. Extemporaneous dispersion/suspension via feeding tube or splanchnic oedema may lower `F1` and increase `IOV`. | Kasichayanula 2011; Penland 2023; DAPA-HF popPK | **Scenario parameters.** Intended for structured sensitivity analysis across clinical phenotypes in the absence of published infant HF popPK data. |
| **9** | **Semi-mechanistic dual PK–PD coupling** | Distinction between luminal SGLT2 occupancy (\(C_{u,50} = 0.20\text{ ng/mL}\)), `GFR`-coupled tubular solute response, and systemic `Emax` relationships. | Explains the clinical dissociation whereby luminal SGLT2 transporters are near-saturated at low GFR (*ceiling effect*), osmotic/natriuretic diuresis is blunted by reduced filtered solute load, yet systemic and glomerular-haemodynamic (TGF) benefits are preserved. | Yakovleva 2014; Yao 2023; Kroonen 2020; FDA NDA 202293 | **Semi-mechanistic.** Based on adult `EAUC50` estimates and proximal-tubule PBPK; postnatal expression ontogeny of renal SGLT2, SGLT1, and NHE3 in neonates remains incompletely quantified. |

#### 2. Mathematical specification of heart failure and PD extensions

1. **Euvolaemic dry weight and heart failure-adjusted clearance (`R/pk_model.R`):**
   $$W_{\text{dry}} = \frac{\text{BWT}}{1 + \text{FO}/100}, \qquad \text{eGFR}_{\text{eff}} = \min\!\left(180, \; \max(5, \; \text{eGFR} \cdot f_{\text{RF}})\right), \qquad \text{RF} = \frac{\text{eGFR}_{\text{eff}}}{\text{nGFR}(\text{AGE}, W_{\text{dry}})}$$
   $$CL_{\text{tv}} = \frac{f_u}{0.086} \cdot \left[5.44 \cdot \text{MAT} \cdot F_{\text{size},CL} \cdot f_{\text{hep}} + 15.78 \cdot \text{MAT} \cdot F_{\text{size},CL} \cdot \text{RF} + 0.011 \cdot \text{eGFR}_{\text{eff}} \cdot \frac{\text{BSA}}{2.0}\right]$$

2. **Unbound chronic steady-state 24-hour exposure (`R/hf_pkpd.R`):**
   $$\text{AUC}_{u,24\text{h,ss},i} = f_{u,i} \cdot \frac{1000 \cdot \text{Dose}_{\text{mg},i} \cdot F_{1,\text{rel}}}{CL_i} \cdot \frac{24}{\tau}$$

3. **Luminal SGLT2 receptor occupancy and GFR-coupled renal PD index:**
   From human SGLT2 \(\text{IC}_{50} = 1.1\text{ nM} = 0.45\text{ ng/mL}\) (`MW = 408.87 g/mol`) and proximal S1/S2 water-reabsorption concentration factor \(\phi_{\text{PT}} = 2.25\), effective \(C_{u,50} = 0.20\text{ ng/mL}\):
   $$O_{\text{SGLT2},i}(t) = \frac{100 \cdot C_{u,i}(t)}{0.20 + C_{u,i}(t)}\text{ (\%)}, \qquad \overline{O}_{\text{SGLT2},i} = \frac{1}{\tau}\int_0^\tau O_{\text{SGLT2},i}(t)\,dt, \qquad \text{PD}_{\text{renal,eff},i} = \overline{O}_{\text{SGLT2},i} \cdot \min\!\left(1.5, \; \frac{\text{eGFR}_{\text{eff},i}}{100}\right)$$

4. **Systemic / cardiorenal exposure–response relationships (Kroonen et al. 2020):**
   $$E_{\text{TGF},i} = \frac{100 \cdot \text{AUC}_{u,24\text{h},i}}{5.2 + \text{AUC}_{u,24\text{h},i}}\text{ (\%)}, \qquad E_{\text{HCT},i} = \frac{100 \cdot \text{AUC}_{u,24\text{h},i}}{20.7 + \text{AUC}_{u,24\text{h},i}}\text{ (\%)}$$
   *(converted from published total-plasma \(\text{EAUC}_{50}\) values of `61 ng·h/mL` for acute glomerular pressure/creatinine response and `241 ng·h/mL` for haematocrit/volume response at adult \(f_u = 0.086\)).*
